A -- THE WEDGE IS A STATEMENT ABOUT ONE RATIO
==============================================================================
  row (3) dies  <=>  w_f/w_g <  27/8 / 13/4 = 27/26
  row (2) lives <=>  w_f/w_g >  27/8 / 23/4 = 27/46

  >> the verdict holds iff   w_f/w_g  in  (27/46, 27/26) = (0.58696, 1.03846)
     which is REPAIR_SPACE.md's diagonal interval, as it must be: on the
     diagonal w_g = 1 and w_f = w.  PASS

  the two margins from the unrescaled point w_f/w_g = 1 are NOT symmetric:
     upward   27/26 - 1 = 1/26 = 3.846 %
     downward 1 - 27/46 = 19/46 = 41.304 %

  CONTROL that had to fire: a COMMON rescaling of both sectors must leave
  the verdict untouched, since it is the sign of a homogeneous expression.
     rescaled by 1/10, 1/2, 1, 3, 100: row (3) dead and row (2) alive in all
     five.  PASS -- so nothing below is about an overall normalisation.

B -- A GAUGE-ONLY TWO-LOOP FACTOR, AND WHERE IT BREAKS THE VERDICT
==============================================================================
  Suppress the gauge sector alone by delta, i.e. w_g = 1 - delta, w_f = 1.
  The ratio becomes 1/(1-delta), and it leaves the wedge when
     1/(1-delta) = 27/26   <=>   delta = 1 - 26/27 = 1/27

  >> THE VERDICT TOLERATES A GAUGE-ONLY SUPPRESSION OF AT MOST 1/27 = 3.7037 %
     Beyond it row (3) survives the donation too, and case (2) stops being
     unique.  (It is the SAME 1/26 margin as above, written the other way.)

  The only estimate of that factor anybody has is the pure-gauge two-loop
  ratio of Dumitru-Guo-Korthals Altes, 5 g^2 C2(A)/(16 pi^2), with C2(A) = 7
  for SU(7).  Against the threshold:

  g_4      delta          ratio 1/(1-d)  verdict
  0.55     0.0670         1.0719         OUTSIDE -- verdict flips
  0.63     0.0880         1.0965         OUTSIDE -- verdict flips
  0.70     0.1086         1.1218         OUTSIDE -- verdict flips
  0.80     0.1418         1.1653         OUTSIDE -- verdict flips

  >> the estimate is 6.70 % to 14.19 % over the plausible coupling range,
     against a threshold of 3.70 %.  Between 1.8 and 3.8 times the margin,
     at every value of g_4.

==============================================================================
WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SAY
==============================================================================
  It does NOT say the verdict is wrong.  The fermion sector receives its own
  two-loop correction, Guo-Du show it is not the same multiple, and nobody
  has computed either one for this model.  If the two track each other to
  within 4 % the ratio never moves and the wedge never notices.

  What it says is that the wedge of REPAIR_SPACE.md is a ONE-LOOP robustness
  statement.  It absorbs any reweighting of the fermion representations, and
  it absorbs any common rescaling; it does not absorb a relative shift
  between the fermion and gauge sectors larger than 1/27.  Loop order is the
  one candidate CROSS_PAPER.md section 3 leaves open, and it is exactly the
  direction the wedge is thinnest in.

  The bounded question that would close it is already stated in the paper:
  whether the two-loop fermionic potential depends on the representation only
  through the multiset of its charges.  TWOLOOP_WEIGHT.md answers that in the
  negative, which is why the ratio cannot be assumed to be 1.
