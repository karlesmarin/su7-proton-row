==============================================================================
BOTH CONDITIONS, NOT ONE -- AND THE WINDOW IN w
==============================================================================
  The paper's claim is not 'case (2) is the only row with D > 0'. It is that
  case (2) is the only row satisfying BOTH necessary conditions. The nu_R
  condition of the anomaly section admits only rows (2) and (3), and carries
  no w at all: it is anomalies and tensor content. So the question is
  narrower than the sweep above --

      for which w does the vacuum condition keep (2) and kill (3)?

  D_i(w) - cost(w) is LINEAR in w, so this is two inequalities and the answer
  is an interval with exact rational endpoints.

  case (2) after donating:  D = 59 - 151/2 w
  case (3) after donating:  D = 37 - 99/2 w

  (2) survives while w < 118/151
  (3) is killed once w >= 74/99

  >> the headline holds exactly on   w in [74/99, 118/151)   =   [0.7475, 0.7815)
     the published reading sits at w = 3/4 = 0.7500, which is inside: True
     margin to the lower edge 0.0025, to the upper edge 0.0315

  And now the exponents, read off that interval rather than re-swept:
     P = 4   w = 1/2     case (3) also survives
     P = 5   w = 3/4     HEADLINE HOLDS
     P = 6   w = 7/8     no row survives at all
     P = 7   w = 15/16   no row survives at all
     P = 8   w = 31/32   no row survives at all

==============================================================================
VERDICT
==============================================================================
  1. The selection is NOT exponent-free. It holds on w in [74/99, 118/151), and of
     the admissible exponents only P = 5 lands inside: at P = 4 case (3)
     survives too and the row is not unique; at P >= 6 NO row survives at all
     and the minimal extension dies outright. So their eq. (67) -- which we
     prove rather than assume, the brace closing to 3k/4(pi n)^5 with Sage
     simplifying the difference to exactly 0 -- is load-bearing for the
     SELECTION and not only for the instrument.
     Section 7 files that proof under 'what the anchor failure cannot be'.
     It belongs in section 5's dependency list too, and it is not there.

  2. And the tight-looking lower margin of 0.0025 is NOT a fragility, which is
     worth stating because it would be easy to sell it as one. w is not a
     free parameter that could drift into the gap: w = 1 - 2^(1-p) takes only
     the values 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, ..., and the nearest admissible neighbours of
     3/4 are 1/2 below and 7/8 above -- both far outside the window.
     The window is narrow in a continuum that the
     physics does not live in. What CAN move the conclusion is a change to
     (A, B) -- new terms, two loops -- and that is the repair wedge of
     section 8, which is measured there and is a different quantity.

  So: one genuine gap in the ledger, and one alarming-looking number that is
  an artifact of sweeping a quantised constant continuously. Both had to be
  computed to tell them apart.

  What this does NOT rescue: alpha_min itself. The exponent is exactly the
  kind of structural difference that could produce a row-dependent ratio,
  and this script does not test that -- it tests only the SELECTION. The
  anchor question stays open and stays where section 7 leaves it.
