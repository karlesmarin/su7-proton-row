THE HYPOTHESIS THIS SCRIPT WAS WRITTEN TO TEST IS REFUTED, AND THAT IS THE
RESULT
==============================================================================
  The hypothesis was: D(48) = 0 says the origin cannot see the 48, so the
  alpha_min column probably cannot either, and w(48) = 5.59 would then be
  the flat direction a least-squares fit runs into rather than a measured
  value.  It would have made leg (b) of the 48 reading worth little.

  Half of it is right and it does not matter.  The 48 IS the softest
  singular direction -- 99 % of it, at a singular value 75 times below
  the stiffest.  So the geometry is exactly as suspected.

  The conclusion drawn from it is wrong twice over:
   - the residual profile has a genuine MINIMUM at 5.59, not a plateau:
     0.0064 there against 0.0102 at 6.0, 0.047 at 8.0 and 0.109 at 0;
   - and propagating the only noise the data actually has -- their two
     significant figures -- moves it only to [5.20, 6.00].

  >> A soft direction is only badly measured relative to the noise, and here
     the noise is two decimal places of somebody's table.  Softest of four
     still means determined to 14 %.  So the alpha_min column DEMANDS
     w(48) = 5.59 and excludes 1; it does not merely tolerate it.

  What the hypothesis got wrong is worth keeping.  D = 0 is one number, the
  curvature AT the origin.  It says nothing about the function away from it,
  and the 48's f'(alpha) at alpha ~ 0.02-0.08 is not small.  A multiplet the
  VERDICT cannot see can be perfectly visible to the MINIMUM.  They are
  different probes of the same potential -- which is the whole reason the
  conclusion of the paper is built on the first one.
