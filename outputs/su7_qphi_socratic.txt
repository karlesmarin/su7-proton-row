A1 -- is the survival condition right?
==============================================================================
  An operator of charge A is made invariant by dressing with n insertions of
  <phi> (charge +q_phi) or <phi*> (charge -q_phi):  A + n q_phi = 0 for some
  integer n of either sign  <=>  A/q_phi in Z.  Two ways it could be wrong:

  (i)  phi REAL.  A real scalar cannot carry a U(1) charge at all -- a real
       representation of U(1) is the trivial one -- so a U(1)'-breaking
       scalar is necessarily complex and both signs are available.
  (ii) the residual group.  U(1) acts on charge Q by exp(i theta Q).  <phi>
       survives iff theta q_phi in 2 pi Z, so the unbroken set is
       theta in (2 pi / q_phi) Z, which acts on charge A by exp(2 pi i A/q_phi).
       Invariant for every element iff A/q_phi in Z.  Same condition.

  >> A1: the condition is the definition, not a model.  SURVIVES.

A4 -- a SECOND breaking scalar, which the paper also does not exclude
==============================================================================
  Two VEVs of charges q1, q2 leave the subgroup that fixes BOTH, i.e. the
  one generated by gcd(q1, q2).  So protection needs A_j / gcd not in Z --
  strictly harder.  The worst case for the strict-compatible solution:

  charges present        gcd        generations dressable
  3/2                    3/2        PROTECTED
  3/2, 1                 1/2        [0, 1, 2]
  3/2, 1/2               1/2        [0, 1, 2]
  3/2, 1, 1/2            1/2        [0, 1, 2]

  >> adding the 7's scalar to the 84's destroys the protection the 84 gave.
     So the claim is not 'the 84 protects' but 'the 84 protects IF it is the
     only U(1)'-breaking VEV'.  A4 does not kill the result; it names the
     hypothesis the result needs, and su7_qphi.py already states it under
     NOT CLAIMED.  SURVIVES, with the scope written in.

A5 -- exact, or only a leading-order selection rule?
==============================================================================
  The residual subgroup of a spontaneously broken GAUGE U(1) is a discrete
  GAUGE symmetry (Krauss-Wilczek 1989), not an accidental global one.  It is
  therefore not violated by gravity or by instantons, and it holds at every
  order in <phi>/M.  This is the whole reason the mechanism is used in the
  literature (JHEP 07 (2008) 065; hep-ph/0012092) and the reason the answer
  to their question can be 'forbidden' rather than 'suppressed'.

  What it does NOT cover, and the note must say so: operators of dimension
  higher than 6, whose charges are different and were not enumerated here;
  and any charge assignment for fields beyond the ones their eqs. (43)-(47)
  and (76) put in the theory.

  >> A5: SURVIVES with scope.
